## Design Specificity Verdict

The landing page feels authored for TaleSpark: its owl identity, illustrated ribbon, and “good questions go places” message fit curiosity-led learning for ages 10–18. That character fades in the tools. The landing has its own styles, the story studio has a denser dashboard system, teacher prep uses another component set, and account/history/story pages lean on utility classes. This makes the branded landing feel like a doorway into several loosely connected applications.

The deterministic scan reported 5 findings: 1 advisory and 4 warnings. It flagged one decorative grid background in `app/globals.css:345`, three overused-font matches (`Arial`) in `app/landing/globals.css:48`, `:55`, and `:111`, and one side-tab accent in `app/teacher/teacher.css:1`. The grid utility appears unused. The Arial uses are scoped to decorative glyphs/ticker or small text, not the main brand type. The side-tab rule is on a review-note paragraph, not a card. These are likely context false positives rather than high-impact user-facing problems.

No overlay was injected. Browser inspection tools available in this run expose read-only page evaluation, so a user-visible overlay could not be verified.

## Overall Impression

TaleSpark has a clear promise, distinctive landing page, and a good story preview. Its biggest opportunity is to make the working product feel like one coherent service while protecting learner work and simplifying the teacher’s first task. The shared language selector is visible but currently sits in a separate top strip, apart from each page’s navigation and the separate language choice for generated content.

## What’s Working

- **Clear learner promise:** “Turn a big question into a little story” is concrete and inviting.
- **Useful product preview:** The Silk Road script lets students and teachers assess the experience before registration.
- **Good workflow feedback:** Story review before video generation, teacher upload limits, and a privacy reminder provide useful confidence and guardrails.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Starting a new story can discard the current draft.** In `app/create/dashboard-client.tsx`, the new-story handler clears the saved draft and resets the script. Offer a save/replace choice or a recoverable draft shelf, and make replacement explicit. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden`.
2. **[P1] The visual system fragments after the landing page.** The landing, story studio, teacher pages, and account/history pages use distinct headers, typography, spacing, and components. Define shared page-shell, type, spacing, button, card, and focus rules, adapted to learner and teacher modes. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable document`.
3. **[P2] Interface and content language controls are disconnected.** The shared interface selector is in its own 52px bar above all pages, while story/material language is a separate form setting. Integrate interface-language selection with each page family’s navigation and label content language as the language of generated material. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout`.
4. **[P2] First-time teacher setup presents many decisions at once.** The lesson form asks for subject, grade, topic, material language, objectives, and source text, followed by four resource choices. Keep required setup first, reveal optional context progressively, and add examples for unfamiliar choices. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard`.
5. **[P2] Long translations need a page-family responsive pass.** Russian and Kazakh have some landing/studio CSS adjustments, but the account, history, story, and teacher surfaces use different sizing and wrapping rules. Check long headings, button widths, and labels at narrow widths in all three languages. **Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt`.

## Cognitive Load

Moderate. The story studio groups key decisions and hides language/voice controls under a disclosure, which helps. The avoidable burden is distinguishing interface language from generated-content language, then learning different navigation and visual patterns as users move between learner and teacher work.

## Persona Red Flags

- **First-time 13-year-old learner:** The landing promise and example are clear. In the story studio, they must distinguish interface language from story language and choose age, duration, and topic. The source-inferred new-story flow risks losing the draft they just shaped.
- **Kazakh- or Russian-speaking mobile learner:** Live checks confirmed the landing and sign-in pages localize. Internal pages have not had a full live phone-width pass; varying page shells and long translations are the main risks.
- **Teacher between classes:** Lessons, source library, retry, refresh, and print actions are discoverable in source. The form asks for many kinds of context at once, and a long resource editor needs a persistent, clear save state and focus.

## Minor Observations

- Registration asks users to choose learner or teacher, while teacher mode also has a self-selection step. Clarify how those choices relate.
- The desktop landing headline dominates the view. Keep enough space for the CTA and supporting line when it wraps in Russian and Kazakh.
- Teacher navigation repeats the same links without an active-page indicator.
- Browser checks showed `/create` and `/teacher` stuck on loading in the available local preview, while the login form stayed disabled at “Signing in…”. Those protected-flow visuals could not be assessed reliably in that environment; treat this as a verification limitation, not yet a product defect.

## Questions to Consider

1. Should the shared product lean toward the playful landing, the quieter story studio, or a deliberate blend with distinct learner and teacher modes?
2. Which area should lead: draft safety, cross-page consistency, or simplifying teacher preparation?
3. Should the next design pass address all five issues or focus on the top three?
